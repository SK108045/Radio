# RIENG Radio Direct Website Advertising Prospecting Report

## Executive view

For RIENG’s specific objective—paid display/banner/in-content advertising on riengradio.co.ke and rieng254.com rather than affiliate-only traffic deals—the best prospects are not simply the biggest Kenyan brands. The strongest targets are companies that either already buy Kenyan digital media, publicly maintain advertising/partnership teams, or operate media-buying/programmatic infrastructure that can place client campaigns onto independent publisher inventory. The clearest immediate opportunities I found are 1xBet, Suss Ads, 22Bet, SportPesa, dentsu Kenya/Africa, Eskimi, SportyBet, Radabet, Bangbet, and Gamemania.

There is one important distinction to keep in mind. 1xBet has an explicitly published “Public relations and advertising” mailbox and a separate online-partnership mailbox, which is unusually good for your use case. Suss Ads explicitly invites media owners/publishers and operates programmatic display campaigns in Kenya, making it potentially even more valuable than another single bookmaker because it can bring multiple advertisers. By contrast, SportyBet, Radabet, and 22Bet Partners publicly emphasize affiliate/CPA/revenue-share arrangements, so your first message to them should explicitly say you are proposing fixed-fee/CPM display inventory and not applying for an affiliate programme.

Kenya’s regulatory position also matters. The Gambling Regulatory Authority’s 2026 regulations sit under the Gambling Control Act framework, while the regulator’s publicly referenced 2025/26 operator register contains many of the brands below. Because Kenya was transitioning licensing arrangements during 2026, an appearance in the 2025/26 list should not be treated as proof that a licence remains valid today; before RIENG runs a gambling campaign, ask the advertiser for its current licence details and approved creative/advertising documentation.

RIENG’s sending setup also gives you a more credible outreach posture than a free Gmail address: the DNS export you supplied shows the custom riengradio.co.ke domain with Resend DKIM and DMARC records configured.

## Why Suss deserves special attention

Suss is arguably the single most strategically useful non-bookmaker lead in the research. Its own materials say it operates programmatic display and video, works across African markets, supports regulated gaming campaigns, and has worked with SportPesa, 22Bet and Betika. It also explicitly has a path for media owners to partner with it. That is almost exactly the intermediary RIENG needs: rather than winning one bookmaker at a time, RIENG could potentially become inventory available to Suss’s campaign planners.

I would therefore put Suss, 1xBet and dentsu in a different category from ordinary cold prospects. For them, the objective is not simply “please advertise on RIENG.” It is “can RIENG become part of the Kenyan publisher inventory you can use when your clients need young entertainment audiences?” That changes the economics because a successful supply relationship can result in repeat campaigns from several advertisers.

## What RIENG should prepare before the next batch

The strongest thing RIENG can do now is produce a one-page advertiser data sheet. Do not inflate traffic. Instead, export actual figures for the most recent 28 or 30 days and the most recent 90 days from whatever analytics stack you use. Agencies and serious media buyers will usually care much more about verifiable inventory than a polished claim such as “huge traffic.”

For the two websites, prepare: monthly users, sessions/pageviews, Kenya percentage, Nairobi percentage if available, age/gender only where your analytics reliably provide it, top pages/content categories, mobile-vs-desktop split, ad impressions available per month, standard banner sizes, average viewability if measured, and screenshots showing proposed placement locations. For the wider RIENG ecosystem, you can separately state the publisher-reported 100,000+ average online-radio listener figure and 74,000+ YouTube subscribers, but make clear those channels are supporting proof of brand reach and that the proposed purchase is website display inventory.

A buyer should also receive a simple starting commercial menu rather than “tell us your budget.” For example, build three options around a two-week test, one-month standard package, and high-visibility sponsorship package. Price can be a fixed monthly amount initially; if your impression measurement becomes mature enough, you can also quote CPM. Fixed-fee packages are entirely credible in this market: Betcheza publicly advertises Kenyan banner packages priced monthly, showing that local digital publishers do sell inventory this way.

The media sheet should identify the seller as:

RIENG Radio  
partnerships@riengradio.co.ke

Your current DNS export supports the use of the RIENG domain for authenticated outbound mail through Resend, including a Resend DKIM record and DMARC policy.

For gambling advertisers, build a separate compliance box into the media sheet: 18+ audiences only, no youth-targeted creative, advertiser supplies current Kenyan licence information and approved creative, responsible-gambling messaging included where required, RIENG reserves the right to reject non-compliant creative. The 2026 Kenyan gambling framework places the sector under the Gambling Regulatory Authority, and responsible-gambling warnings already appear prominently on current operator sites such as Bangbet and betPawa.

## Recommended sending sequence

I would not send all of these in one afternoon. The best sequence is Suss → 1xBet → 22Bet → SportPesa → dentsu → Eskimi → SportyBet → Radabet → Bangbet → Gamemania, with the smaller Wekelea/agency/network prospects following afterward. That ordering reflects likelihood of reaching an actual advertising decision-maker plus potential deal value, rather than company size.

The first wave should be highly personalized. Suss should receive an inventory-partnership message. 1xBet should receive a direct advertising message because it has an explicit marketing mailbox. 22Bet/SportyBet/Radabet should receive a non-affiliate direct-buy message. SportPesa’s media inbox should receive a routing request to digital marketing/media buying. dentsu and Eskimi should receive a publisher onboarding/supply pitch. Those differences matter because sending the same Betika template to every organization would cause the agency and programmatic prospects to misunderstand what you are offering.

For routing-only contacts such as Bangbet, Gamemania and Wekelea, keep the first email to roughly three or four sentences. Do not attach the full media deck yet. Ask for the responsible person first. Their public addresses are customer-support channels, not verified marketing mailboxes.

For direct addresses like marketing@1xbet-team.com, b2b@1xbet-team.com, support@22betpartners.com, partners@sportybet.com, affiliates@mobisphere.co.ke, partners@eskimi.com, and dentsu’s published Africa/new-business addresses, you can send the actual concise RIENG proposal immediately because the sources explicitly associate those routes with partnerships, advertising, publishers, or business development.

## Bottom line

The strongest immediate direct advertiser lead is 1xBet, because it publicly separates advertising and B2B contacts and has an active Kenya presence.

The strongest media intermediary is Suss Ads, because it explicitly works with media owners, buys programmatic display, operates from Nairobi, plans regulated-category campaigns, and publicly references work with SportPesa, 22Bet and Betika.

After those two, 22Bet, SportPesa, dentsu Kenya/Africa, Eskimi, SportyBet and Radabet form the most practical second tier. 22Bet has a documented route for business opportunities outside affiliate marketing; SportPesa has a public media-relations route; dentsu has a Nairobi office and Africa new-business contact; Eskimi has both a Nairobi presence and a publisher-partner address; SportyBet and Radabet have active partner teams, although both should be explicitly told that RIENG wants a paid media buy rather than affiliate commission.

The smaller Kenyan brands—Bangbet, Gamemania and Wekelea—are worth contacting precisely because they may have less bureaucratic procurement than major operators, but their publicly verifiable email routes are currently support-oriented, so the first goal with them is obtaining the correct marketing decision-maker rather than sending a long proposal into customer care.

The prospecting strategy I would use for RIENG is therefore not “find 100 sportsbook emails.” It is to build a tight list of roughly 15–20 verified buyers and intermediaries, separate direct media buyers from affiliate programmes, and establish one or two repeatable agency/programmatic relationships alongside direct bookmaker deals. That gives RIENG a much better chance of turning its two websites into recurring paid inventory rather than chasing one-off affiliate arrangements.
